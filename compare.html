SpaceWise vs. the alternatives

Last checked 13 August 2026 · prices taken from each vendor's own site

How to read this page. Every competitor here is a real, well-made product, and two of them are better than SpaceWise at the thing they're built for. This page says so explicitly, because a comparison that finds the author winning every row isn't a comparison — it's an advert. Prices change; the links go straight to each vendor so you can check.

The short version

DaisyDisk

$9.99 one-time · their pricing

The best visual map of your disk, full stop. A gorgeous sunburst that makes big files obvious in seconds, and it excludes clones and hard links so its numbers are honest.

Where it stops: it shows you where space went, not what any of it is. Faced with ~/Library/Caches/com.apple.iconservices at 3 GB, you still have to know whether deleting it is fine. That knowledge is exactly the gap SpaceWise fills.

CleanMyMac

From $3.33/month, subscription · their site

The most complete suite: cleaning, malware scanning, an uninstaller, performance tools, duplicate finder, cloud cleanup. Polished, notarized, and backed by a large company.

Where it stops: it's a subscription, it's a much bigger surface than "find my disk space," and its cleaning is largely one-button — you're trusting the verdict rather than reading the reasoning.

macOS Storage settings

Free, already installed

Built in, safe, and enough for many people. Offloads unused apps, empties Trash automatically, shows large files.

Where it stops: the "System Data" bucket is famously unexplained, and it can't see most developer or AI caches at all. This is the gap that made us build SpaceWise: 65 GB reported under "Applications," 11 GB actually in the folder.

Honestly: if you want the prettiest map of your disk and you already know what your folders are, buy DaisyDisk — it's $9.99 and it's excellent. If you want one app that also removes malware, uninstalls apps and finds duplicates, CleanMyMac does more than we do and always will. SpaceWise is for the case in between: you can see something is eating your disk, you don't recognise it, and you're not willing to click Clean on a folder you can't identify.

Where SpaceWise is genuinely worse right now

  • Not notarized yet. macOS will warn you, and you'll need System Settings to get past it. The others all open cleanly. Here's the install guide.
  • No visual sunburst map. If spatial browsing is how you think, DaisyDisk will suit you better.
  • Narrower scope. No malware scanning, no uninstaller, no duplicate finder. That's deliberate — but if you want a suite, it's a real gap.
  • Version 1.0. DaisyDisk has been refined since 2008 and CleanMyMac has a company behind it. We have a rules database, a safety engine and a test suite — but not a decade of edge cases.
  • 63 rules is a starting point. It covers the big modern space-eaters well. It does not know every app on earth, and anything it doesn't recognise is reported as unidentified rather than guessed at.

Where SpaceWise is genuinely better

  • It tells you what things are. Every item gets an explanation of what it is, why it exists and what breaks if it goes. No other tool here does this per item.
  • It refuses to delete things it can't justify. Unrecognised means "review first," never "safe." Credentials, cloud-synced folders and personal media can't be one-click deleted at all, whatever the rules say.
  • It knows what actually fills a 2026 Mac. Model weights and build caches are where the tens of gigabytes are now, and generic cleaners haven't caught up.
  • The privacy claim is checkable in a minute. One network request exists in the entire codebase. Run Little Snitch and confirm it — you don't have to trust a policy page.
  • One-time pricing, and honest about it. $29 once. Everything works if you never pay; the reminder is dismissible and never blocks anything.
